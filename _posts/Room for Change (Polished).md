"*Finally, i got the chance to move out of that damn house,*" Raela thought to herself as she got in her dorm room.

The dorm room, surprisingly, met her expectations and requests: third floor, a single wide window with a view of a big tree outside. Sunlight filtered through the leaves before spilling gently into the room. The staff had told her they couldn’t promise anything, so she hadn’t held out much hope. But luck seemed to be on her side, for once.

The room had two single beds lined up against opposite walls, each with its own desk. Between them sat a small bedside table with a reed diffuser on top. It smelled like jasmine.

“*Not bad*,” Raela thought. She preferred the scent of wood, but at least it wasn’t vanilla—that would’ve made her dizzy.

She sat on the left bed and felt the softness. Then, without unpacking, she lay down and stared at the ceiling with her round glasses still on, her suitcase and backpack still untouched on the floor.

“*Now what*?” she wondered.

She’d finally escaped. Finally living on her own. But it felt... empty.  
No spark. No joy.

She had expected this to help. To heal her, maybe.  
But she felt nothing.

The room was nice, everything she asked for. She should be happy.  
So why wasn’t she?

“*Have twelve years of suffering really broken me this bad?*”

She was five when her parents died. She was there when it happened—when the treehouse they built together collapsed. They used to play there almost every day. She could still see the image clearly: the shattered wood, her parents crushed under a fallen log.

She never believed it was an accident. Not after what she saw. Her aunt had found them first, looked down at their broken bodies… then smiled. Covered her mouth like she was shocked, right in front of Raela’s eyes. But she was just a child. What could she do?

She never spoke again after that. People assumed it was because she was hurt—maybe her throat, maybe her head. Some kind of injury. The doctors couldn’t find anything, but no one pushed further. It was easier to say “something broke” than to ask _what_.

But Raela knew. There was no injury.  
She just... stopped.

Not out of pain. Not fear.

She just didn’t see the point anymore.

After the funeral, her aunt took over everything. Claimed she’d take care of Raela and “manage” the inheritance. It would’ve been bearable—maybe—if she’d truly cared. But that kindness lasted less than a year.

Then came the neglect. The cruelty.

The abuse.

All while living comfortably on money that wasn’t hers.

Raela had no one—no other family, no friends, no help. Eventually, she stopped reacting to the pain. It became background noise. Those twelve years blurred together, not a single moment worth holding on to.

Twelve years should’ve felt short for her kind. But for her personally, it felt like a century.

Getting that scholarship was the only break she’d ever gotten. A godsend.

And yet... here she was, lying in her new bed, still feeling the same.

---

A knock on the door yanked her from her thoughts.

She stood and opened it.

A guy—a human—stood there. Short beard, round glasses, messy short dark hair. He wasn’t that tall, but still maybe ten or fifteen centimeters taller than her. She watched him, confused.

“Umm... sorry to disturb you,” he said, scratching the back of his neck. “But is this room 342?”

She nodded slowly.

“Do you, like... occupy this room?” he asked.

She nodded again, even slower. He looked more confused.

“Are you a male?” he asked.

She blinked at him, then shook her head.

“Oh. Were you a male, by any chance?”

“*What is this guy on about?*” she thought, frowning. She shook her head again, more firmly this time.

“This is strange,” he muttered. “Are you _sure_ you are or were not a male?”

*Do i look like a male?* Raela's thought.
Pale skin. Ash-blonde hair. a layered bob with curtain bangs. Small nose. Titanium stud on her pointy ear. Whole face small and kinda soft. Sure, the oversized hoodie made her look androgynous, maybe. But her face? Come on. No way anyone with a functioning brain would—

_Fuck this guy._

She stared at him. Then closed the door in his face.

“Look,” he said from the other side, sounding a bit guilty, “I might sound rude, but the dorm staff gave me this room, and I specifically requested a male roommate. I was just making sure, because you definitely don’t look like a guy.”

She opened the door again and signed: _I’m not._

He squinted. “Uhh... is that a gang sign?”

“*Fucking hell, this guy.*” she thought.

She turned around, stomped to her backpack, grabbed a marker and a sheet of paper, and wrote:

**I am not a male.**

His eyes widened. He blinked, eyes flickering between confusion and concern. “Wait... you can’t talk?” His voice softened, careful now, like he didn’t want to mess up. “Damn. I’m really sorry. I—I didn’t mean to offend you or anything. I’ll, uhh, go talk to the staff. Sorry for taking your time. Really.”

He practically fled down the hallway.

Raela sighed and dropped back onto her bed.
“*First day and a problem already? Even here, I can’t get some peace, huh?*”
Not long after, she drifted off into a light nap.

---

Diin stormed down the hallway, holding back his frustration. He blamed the dorm staff—at least a little. If they hadn’t screwed up the room assignment, he wouldn’t have gone through that painfully awkward convo. And he definitely wouldn’t have come off as rude to someone who literally can’t speak.

He took a deep breath, trying to calm down as he reached the front desk.

"Excuse me, I think you assigned me the wrong room. Could you please check?" he asked, keeping it polite.

Unfortunately, the afternoon shift was run by a girl who clearly didn’t wanna be there. She didn’t even look at him—just stuck her hand out like she was asking for a tip.

Diin blinked. "Umm... do you need something?"

She sighed hard. “ID and key. Do I _really_ have to say that? Can’t you think for yourself?”

Diin almost snapped right there. But instead, he forced a tight smile and handed over what she asked for. “Sorry.”

“Ugh.” She typed on the system like the keyboard owed her money. “Diin Santara, yeah? Room 342. That _is_ your room. So... are we done wasting my time?”

Diin frowned. “But... there’s already a girl in that room.”

She didn’t even flinch. “Yeah? Bad luck. We don’t do solo rooms unless you’re paying triple. Take your ID and key.”

“I _know_ it’s not a solo room. But I requested a _male_ roommate.” He rubbed his temples. “Can I talk to someone else? Because your attitude isn’t helping.”

She gave him a sarcastic smile. “Aww. Wish granted. I'm here 'til morning.”

Diin sighed. “Then... can you at least _try_ to help?”

The staff girl shrugged. “It’s just a room, dude. Chill. What’s the big deal? Scared of girls? Or do you have a history of... something worse?”

Diin stiffened. “What the hell—no, I’m just—”

She cut him off. “Can’t help. Just sleep there for tonight. Talk to someone who actually gives a shit in the morning.”

“But I—"

“You’re not one of those guys who _assault people in their sleep_, right?” she added with a fake chuckle. “Unless... oh wait, are you gay? Is that why you asked for a male room-”

Diin slammed his palm on the desk before the staff couldn't finish her roast. “For fuck’s sake—just _shut up_ if you’re not gonna help. God damn it.” He snatched his ID and key off the desk, turning away.

Behind him, the staff’s tone softened a bit. “Look, I’m sorry, okay? I’m just covering for someone and I’m not trained for this. It’s been a rough day.”

Diin stopped. He looked back at her and sighed. “It’s alright. I'm sorry too. Didn’t mean to lose it.”

He walked off, back to his room.

---

He stood in front of the door for a while, just staring. “*Am I really about to spend the night in a room with a random girl?*”

His beliefs said no. He wasn’t even supposed to be _alone_ in a room with a woman he wasn’t bonded to—let alone _sleeping_ under the same roof.

He stepped back until his shoulders hit the hallway wall, then slid down to sit. What was he supposed to do? He didn’t know anyone here. Didn’t have a backup plan. And on top of that, their first meeting had already been a disaster.

He rubbed his face, like maybe that’d help him think straight. He could sleep outside. Or ask someone to swap. Or…
He stared at the key in his hand.

As he sat in silence, trying to think things through—

_Click._

The door opened.

---

Raela stepped out of the room with a small plastic bag in hand, filled with used toiletries she had cleaned up from the bathroom after waking from her nap. It honestly baffled her—The fact that the dorm staff hadn’t cleaned the bathroom properly before handing the room over? Gross. It made her stomach churn. _Unbelievable._

She paused, tilting her head slightly when she spotted Diin sitting on the hallway floor, back leaned against the wall like he was in some kind of personal crisis. He was looking away, clearly avoiding eye contact. Awkward energy all over him.

She narrowed her eyes for a moment, then decided to ignore him. “*Not my problem*.” She turned on her heel and walked away. She walked off toward the nearest large trash bin on the floor. The smell from the bag followed her until she dumped it into the bin with a loud _clunk_.

On her way back, Diin hadn’t moved. Same spot. Same posture.

She narrowed her eyes. _Weird._

She stepped past him, opened the door, went inside, and let it close behind her with a soft click. Throwing herself back on the bed, she stared at the ceiling.

_Is it okay to ignore him?_ she wondered. _He doesn't look okay… but again, not my business._

She sighed and rolled over. _Fuck._ Her brow furrowed. Her heart beat a little faster, not out of concern—but out of _annoyance_. _Why’s he just sitting out there?_ she thought. _They didn’t give him a new room? Then what the hell is he waiting for?_

After a moment, she sat up, pushed herself off the bed, and walked out. She nudged him lightly with her foot.

His head snapped up like he’d just been caught stealing. She signed slowly, almost like she was tired: _What are you doing?_ Her eyes flickered with a hint of annoyance— like she was trying to decide if she should care.

As expected, he didn’t understand.

“I’m sorry… I don’t understand,” he said quietly.

Raela sighed and tilted her head up and down, a bit exaggerated, like she was already exhausted by this whole thing.

She walked back to the door, yanked it wide open, and waved him over with a slight gesture, thumb pointing into the room. The silent universal sign for _Are you coming in or what?_

Diin hesitated, then stood up slowly.

His feet stayed glued to the floor for a second too long. For a flash of a second, his brain threw him back years—to when he was thirteen, sitting in his room with a childhood friend. A girl. They were just playing some dumb board game. His mom had walked in, seen the closed door, and gone ballistic.
“Never close the door if there’s a girl inside,” she’d snapped. “I don’t care if she’s your friend. It is prohibited. People talk.”

He shuffled his feet, voice low and hesitant. “I’m coming in… but would it be okay to leave the door open?” His voice cracked a little. Nervous.

Raela stared at him without blinking for a few seconds, then nodded slowly while looking away. She turned and walked inside.

“Umm… thanks,” he mumbled as he followed her in.

Raela sat down on her bed while Diin dropped his suitcase by the door to hold it open—the door closer would’ve shut it otherwise. His eyes darted around the room—left bed, right bed, desks, diffuser. He then sat down on the right bed.

“It’s a comfy room, isn’t it?” he asked, trying to sound casual.

She didn’t answer, just stared at him. Blank.

“Sorry about earlier,” he continued quickly. “I really didn’t mean to offend you or anything. Also, thanks for letting me in. And… sorry for asking to leave the door open. I know it’s weird.”

Raela looked away, raising her eyebrows slightly in a _whatever_ gesture.

Diin let out a small breath and started telling her what happened with the front desk—how he probably had no choice but to spend the night here.

She didn’t say anything. Just gave him a slow thumbs-up, expression flat as ever.

“I’m Diin, by the way. Diin Santara.” He scratched his neck. “Not like I’m trying to force some friendship or anything—I mean, doesn’t mean I _don’t_ want to, but maybe you’re not comfortable with that… I just—” He groaned under his breath. “Whatever, never mind. I don’t know what I’m saying.”

He looked away, clearly embarrassed.

Raela grabbed a marker and a scrap of paper from her desk. Without saying a word, she wrote something down and held it up.

**Raela Ordhorn.**

He leaned closer. “Raela Ordhorn? Raela? Is that your name?”

She nodded slightly, eyes lowering for a second.

“…Nice to meet you, Raela.”

---

After minutes of silence between them—though to Diin, it felt like hours—he finally spoke up.

“Are you an elf? Sorry, I mean… is it okay to ask?”

Raela nodded.

“Wait, sorry—which one are you nodding for?”

Raela signed, _Both_.

Diin noticed she held up two fingers in the motion. “Oh… does that mean _both_?”

Raela raised her eyebrows and nodded again, this time with a hint of amusement in her eyes. A flicker of energy.

Diin grinned, genuinely pleased. “Nice.”

He relaxed a little more. “Actually, I’ve never seen an elf before… let alone talked to one.”

Raela squinted at him, tilted her head, then lightly scratched the air with her index finger.

Diin furrowed his brow. “Alright, I don’t get that one.”

But he chuckled softly. “I’m guessing you’re surprised? There are only humans where I’m from. Elves are more like… myths. And honestly, people don’t talk about them positively.”

Raela rolled her eyes.

“Not that I believe that stuff,” Diin added quickly. “I’m lucky, I guess. My mom raised me better than that. She never taught me to hate anyone, especially not a whole race.”

Raela gave him a long stare—like she wasn’t fully buying it—but Diin looked away, pretending not to notice.

“Anyway, I think I should explore a bit. Maybe there’s another place I can crash for the night. I still wanna try not sleeping here if I can help it. But thanks for letting me come in earlier. I’m a bit calmer now.”

Raela signed, _Why are you trying that hard?_

Diin winced, flashing an awkward grin while pointing at himself. “Yeahhh… still don’t understand sign language.”

Raela sighed and grabbed her marker again. Before she could write anything down, Diin asked, “Is it okay if I leave my suitcase here while I check around?”

She looked up at him, standing near the door already. Then closed her eyes briefly and nodded once.

“Thanks. See you later, I guess,” Diin said as he walked out.

Raela watched him leave, her expression unreadable. Once his step faded, she sighed and tossed the marker onto her desk.

She dropped back onto her bed, arms sprawled, eyes closed.

_“What did I get myself into?”_

Diin walked along the hallway, deep in thought, wondering where the hell he could crash tonight.

His best shot was probably the common area—_if_ the dorm even had one.  
Another option? Find a guy with no roommate. Or at least someone whose roommate wasn’t around tonight. Worst case, maybe someone would let him sleep on the floor… if that was allowed.

He kept walking, eyes scanning the doors, until he saw a guy unlocking one. Diin quickly approached.

“Excuse me, do you have a roommate?”

The guy jumped slightly, startled. “Are you... talking to me?”

“Well yeah,” Diin said casually, glancing around. “No one else here.”

“Oh—uh, yeah. I do. Why?” the guy fumbled with his keys, clearly flustered.

“Is he not here tonight, by any chance?” Diin asked, hopeful.

“Actually... no. Why?”

“I’ve got a situation in my room. Can’t sleep there tonight. Would it be alright if I crashed here just for one night?”

The guy blinked. “Oh wow. This is... new.”

“New?”

“Yeah—I mean, no one’s ever asked me that way before.” The guy was blushing now.

Diin raised an eyebrow. “Wait… what?”

“You’re pretty creative, you know,” the guy said, laughing nervously. “Didn’t expect someone to hit on me like that.”

He reached out and lightly brushed Diin’s arm.

Diin stepped back, alarmed. “Bro, nope. I think you got the wrong idea. I’m not trying to sleep _with_ you. I just needed a place to crash. Not... whatever this is.”

The guy giggled. “Are you roleplaying right now? I _love_ that.”

Diin stared blankly. “Get well soon, brother.” He turned and started walking off.

“Wait, you can’t just leave after hitting on me like that!” the guy called out.

But Diin didn’t respond. He just kept walking, face deadpan, soul already halfway out of his body.
